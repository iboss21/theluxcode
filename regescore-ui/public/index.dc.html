QUESTION
What compliance obligations apply if we host client documents for EU customers on our own hardware?
FINDINGS
01
Self-hosting does not exempt you from GDPR — you remain the processor, and Article 28 terms still need to be in every client contract. [3][7]
02
Keeping data on EU hardware removes the transfer-mechanism problem entirely, which is the single largest source of audit findings in this category. [1][9]
03
ISO 27001 is not legally required but is what enterprise procurement will ask for; SOC 2 Type II covers the same ground for US-headquartered buyers. [4][11]
04
Sources disagree on retention defaults — two argue 30 days is defensible, one insists on client-specified only. Flagged for legal review rather than resolved. [2][6][13]
NOT LEGAL ADVICE
Synthesis only. Route the retention question and the Article 28 template to counsel before anything ships to a client.

Debate

CALL VERDICT
MOTION90 seconds of shard lag should be accepted as steady-state, not degraded.ROUND 3 / 5
FOR · ADVERSARY-A
CONF 0.74
R1
Availability is measured on retrieval latency, not replica lag. 90s of lag with sub-250ms reads is, by the runbook definition, nominal.
R2
The alternative — halting writes to guarantee consistency — converts a soft degradation into a hard outage. That trade is strictly worse for the operator.
R3
Precedent: the 30 JUL incident resolved itself in 4m12s with zero user-visible error. Treating it as degraded would have paged three people for nothing.
AGAINST · ADVERSARY-B
CONF 0.68
R1
A stale read is a wrong answer delivered confidently. For ledger queries that is not degradation, it is fabrication with a timestamp.
R2
The runbook definition is the artefact of one incident, not a principle. Citing it here is circular — it was written to excuse exactly this case.
R3
Meridian is disputing £4,120 on a fee-cycle mismatch. If a 90s-stale ledger read informs that reply, the citation article is already violated.
SCORECARD
EVIDENCE · FOR0.74
EVIDENCE · AGAINST0.68
CITED CLAIMS9 / 11
SYNTHESIS
Both sides converge on a split: accept 90s for read-only, non-financial paths; treat any lag above zero as degraded for ledger and contract reads. That distinction is not yet in the runbook.

We measure marginal cost per million tokens across seven consumer accelerators and find that the crossover against hosted inference occurs at 14.2M tokens per month, roughly an order of magnitude lower than 2024 estimates.
